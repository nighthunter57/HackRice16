# Deterministic forecast assumptions

`simulate` and `analyze` are pure, synchronous functions. Amounts and every intermediate sum must be safe integer USD cents. Invalid dates, noninteger money, overflow, cross-user entities, unknown accounts, and horizons outside 1–366 inclusive days throw. Dates use UTC; the horizon includes its start day. Transactions accept date-only UTC values or ISO timestamps ending in `Z`. No clock, remote service, randomness, or language model participates.

Account balances are the opening snapshot before start-day events. Historical transactions are already reflected in that snapshot and are never debited again. Bills and income use positive amounts; transactions use signed amounts. Income defaults to a one-off event; supported recurrences are once, weekly, biweekly and monthly. Monthly dates retain the original day with month-end clamping (January 31 → February 28 → March 31). Purchases are always one-off; purchaseType describes necessity, not recurrence.

Daily event order is income, positive scheduled transactions, bills, negative scheduled transactions, predicted everyday spending, then the proposed purchase. Source array order breaks ties. Opening and intraday balances count toward safety, so income later in the horizon never erases an earlier shortfall. Mandatory bills default to true and are checked at payment time. All listed bills are debited even when optional. Internal transfer transactions have zero effect on pooled account cash. Providers must not represent the same payment in both bills/income and scheduled transactions.

Expected spending uses negative discretionary transactions in the 30 complete UTC days before startDate, divided by 30. Missing history implies zero estimated spending. Remainder cents are distributed across the first days of each 30-day cycle. Scheduled discretionary expenses consume the estimate, leaving any unspent remainder as expected spending; a small known purchase never erases the whole day's estimate. This is a transparent estimate, not a claim that future expenses are known.

Goal savedCents are earmarked assets already included in account balances. Their sum is subtracted once from pooled cash and cannot exceed those assets. The demo's separate $1,900 savings account backs exactly $1,900 of goal reserves. Additional goal funding uses a single shared surplus pool in goal array order, preventing multiple goals claiming the same dollars. Projections use cash above the buffer that remains available through the end of the horizon; hypothetical goal allocations do not also debit the forecast. A null date means not projected within this horizon, not never. A goal's deadline, when supplied, is a hard constraint. A finite baseline completion may slip by maxDelayDays (default zero); slipping beyond the horizon fails. Two unknown dates have unknown delay and do not impose an invented deadline.

SAFE requires no negative cash, all mandatory bills covered, no buffer violation, and satisfied goal constraints. Negative cash, an uncovered mandatory bill, a hard buffer violation, or a missed goal deadline is NOT_RECOMMENDED. Exceeding a goal delay tolerance is CAUTION and is excluded from Safe Date recommendations. Equality with the buffer is safe. Safe dates are searched chronologically across the entire forecast. The wait result is null when today is already safe or no safe date exists. Safe maximum is for buying today and is null when even a zero-cent purchase is unsafe. Price search is monotonic because increasing a one-off price only decreases subsequent cash and cannot accelerate goal funding; integer binary search returns the exact cent boundary. Findings apply only to the supplied horizon and assumptions.

The demo is pinned to September 12, 2026 for 60 days. Its $449 Sony purchase first becomes safe on September 18. Adding one $430 repair bill on September 20 moves the first safe date to September 25 through actual cash flows. injectRepair returns an independent state and is idempotent by bill ID.

The runtime banking provider (`src/data/banking-demo.ts`) uses 60 days of realistic history and additional obligations. Its computed dates are September 17 and September 24 after the repair. The older fixture above remains an algorithm regression test. Emergency and travel savings are reserved through explicit goals, so opening spendable cash is only $1,850.

Every simulation in the analysis response includes `balanceImpact`: current spendable balance, purchase price, balance before and immediately after purchase, forecast minimum, safety buffer, and the signed difference between that minimum and the buffer. Spendable cash is pooled liquid account cash minus earmarked goal savings, before subtracting the safety buffer. For buying today, immediate balance is opening spendable cash minus the price, independently of same-day scheduled events. For a future purchase, the before/after values use that date's projected cash after the engine's prior events; `isFuturePurchase` and `purchaseDate` distinguish it from cash available now. The projected minimum includes opening and intraday balances throughout the entire horizon, including days before a waiting scenario's purchase. Baselines have zero purchase price and a null purchase date. All differences use checked integer-cent arithmetic.
